The problem

Korea already saves this way — on trust alone

The kye (곗돈) is a rotating savings club: friends put in a fixed amount each round, and the whole pot rotates to one member at a time. It's a centuries-old habit — and it runs on nothing but a group chat and a promise.

The organizer holds everyone's money

One dishonest host and the entire pot is gone.

No record, no recourse

Disputes come down to whose word you believe.

Missed payments break the circle

Nothing enforces that everyone keeps paying in.

The insight

The trust is the bug. The contract is the fix.

How it works

Four steps from group chat to trustless savings

1

Create

Set members, amount per round, and the payout order. One transaction spins up a circle.

2

Join

Members connect a browser wallet, approve once, and join by posting a bond. Social login is next.

3

Contribute

Each round, everyone pays the fixed amount into escrow. Miss it and your bond is slashed.

4

Rotate

When the timer ends, anyone closes the round and the contract pays the scheduled member — in about a second on GIWA.

Web2-easy on the surface. On-chain-trustless underneath.

The guarantee, enforced in code

Funds leave a circle only to the round's fixed recipient, or back to a member as their bond. There is no admin function that can move the pot anywhere else — proven by a solvency invariant that held across 128,000 random call sequences.
