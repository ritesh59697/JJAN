# The problem

## Korea already saves this way — on trust alone

The _kye_ (곗돈) is a rotating savings and credit association: a group of friends,
family, or coworkers each put in a fixed amount every round, and the entire pooled
amount rotates to one member at a time until everyone has received the pot once.
It is a centuries-old habit and remains widespread today.

And it runs on nothing but a group chat and a promise.

## Where it breaks

{% hint style="danger" %}
The whole arrangement depends on one person — the organizer — being honest with
everyone else's money. That single point of trust is also its single point of
failure.
{% endhint %}

* **The organizer holds everyone's money.** One dishonest host, and the entire pot
  is gone. This is the classic _kye_ fraud, and it happens.
* **No record, no recourse.** When something goes wrong, disputes come down to
  whose word you believe. There is no shared, tamper-proof ledger of who paid what.
* **Missed payments break the circle.** Nothing enforces that every member keeps
  paying in after they've received their payout — leaving later recipients exposed.

## The opportunity

The _kye_ is one of the most culturally native financial behaviours in Korea, and
it maps almost perfectly onto what a smart contract is good at: holding funds under
transparent rules and releasing them automatically. The trust that makes _kye_
fragile is exactly the thing a contract removes.

That is what [Jjan does](how-it-works.md).
